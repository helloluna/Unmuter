## Executive Summary

Between **2022 and 2025**, the Western Australian **Public Transport Authority (PTA)**, through its **School Bus Services (SBS)** division, authorised and maintained the use of a **mechanical restraint (harness)** on two disabled students — referred to on this site as **Orion** and **Linden** — during daily school transport.

Over a period of **three years**, this restrictive practice was used:

- **Without any therapist or clinical assessment**  
- **Without the mandatory three-monthly reviews** required by policy  
- **Without any documented fade-out or step-down plan**

When the family later requested review and professional oversight, responsibility was repeatedly shifted onto them.  
Multiple oversight bodies — **PTA**, **Ombudsman WA**, **EOC WA**, and the **Australian Human Rights Commission (AHRC)** — either declined to investigate, delayed action, or cited jurisdictional barriers.

The result was a **systemic accountability vacuum**:  
every rule was followed by the family, and **every oversight mechanism failed**.

## What Happened (High-Level Timeline)

- **Mid-2022**  
  SBS approved the use of a chest harness for school transport, citing safety concerns.  
  Approval was issued internally, without occupational therapy or behavioural assessment.

- **2022–2025**  
  The harness remained in use for approximately **three years**.  
  No reviews, no reassessments, and no fade-out planning occurred.

- **July 2024**  
  PTA quietly amended its policy (STAP §7.5) to require a *qualified prescriber* for restraint use — implicitly acknowledging earlier gaps.

- **Early 2025**  
  The family requested formal review and a step-down plan.  
  SBS and PTA responded that **families were responsible** for initiating and funding professional assessments.

- **Aug–Oct 2025**  
  Formal complaints were lodged with Ombudsman WA, EOC WA, and AHRC.  
  Oversight bodies declined or deferred substantive investigation.

## Why This Matters

This case is not about one decision or one officer.  
It exposes a **structural pattern**:

- A government agency authorises a restrictive practice  
- Oversight requirements exist on paper but are not enforced  
- Responsibility for safety, review, and compliance is transferred to families  
- Complaint bodies rely on procedure, time limits, or jurisdiction to avoid intervention  

What appears to be a functioning system is, in practice, **a loop of deflection**.

---

## Core Findings

**Administrative failure**  
PTA acknowledged that no review occurred, yet denied responsibility for ensuring one took place.

**Oversight collapse**  
Ombudsman WA redirected the complaint back to the agency complained about and later reset the case timeline.

**Jurisdictional deadlock**  
State and federal human rights bodies each cited limits that left no authority accountable for the full conduct.

**Human impact**  
While institutions debated process, the children remained subject to an unreviewed restrictive practice.
